Teaching points

1. Why the prior exists at all

Population PK gives a reasonable starting clearance before any levels are drawn, built from age, weight, sex, and renal function via Cockcroft-Gault. It's the best guess available at the moment the first dose is ordered — not a final answer.

2. What the Bayesian update actually does

MAP estimation weighs the observed level against how confident the prior already was. A level that falls close to what the population model predicted barely moves the posterior; a level far outside that range shifts the estimate further, because it's more informative about this specific patient.

3. Where this simplification departs from real software

This tool updates a single parameter — clearance — while holding volume of distribution fixed at the population estimate, to keep the math visible. Production Bayesian dosing software builds on this same core idea but updates multiple parameters jointly, draws on richer covariate models, and uses priors validated against large patient datasets.

Clinical pearl

A level drawn during the distribution phase (too soon after infusion ends) will read artificially high and pull the posterior in the wrong direction. Getting the sample timing right matters as much as the estimation method itself — garbage in, garbage out applies to Bayesian forecasting too.
