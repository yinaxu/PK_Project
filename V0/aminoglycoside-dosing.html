CLINICAL INTERPRETATION
Teaching points

1. High-Dose Extended-Interval Rationale

Aminoglycosides display concentration-dependent killing (optimizing Peak/MIC ratio) and a post-antibiotic effect (PAE). Administering a large single dose (7 mg/kg) maximizes bacterial clearance while long dosing intervals allow concentrations to drop below toxic thresholds.

2. Single Random Level Concept

Instead of drawing classical steady-state peak and trough pairs, the Hartford nomogram relies on a single serum concentration drawn 6 to 14 hours post-infusion. This measurement reflects the patient's individual elimination rate constant (Ke) and maps directly to an appropriate interval.

3. Mathematical Derivation vs. Digitized Nomograms

Traditional nomograms use static paper charts. This tool mathematically reconstructs the underlying boundary lines by back-calculating the exact elimination rate constant (Ke) required to reach a safe trough (< 2 mg/L) at 24, 36, or 48 hours for a 7 mg/kg dose.

Clinical pearls & limitations

Extended-interval dosing is intended for patients with stable renal function. Avoid using this nomogram approach in pregnancy, severe ascites, major burns (>20% BSA), end-stage renal disease/dialysis, or when treating endocarditis where synergy dosing (3 mg/kg/day split Q8h) is indicated.
